# Reference Contract — Industrial Maison Rouge

## 1. Visual thesis

**Industrial Maison Rouge** treats fashion commerce as a piece of spatial infrastructure: severe, modular and materially raw, but still unmistakably editorial and luxurious.

The visual system combines:

- exposed, rectilinear brutalist structure;
- warm editorial restraint inherited from the first successful EXP-01 proposal;
- large fashion imagery;
- black/bone/concrete surfaces;
- a deep crimson interruption;
- a deliberately bounded surreal quality expressed primarily through image composition, scale and unusual spatial tension.

The desired experience is **fashion as structure, tension and desire**, not “brutalism for its own sake.”

## 2. Aesthetic weighting

Use these as directional guardrails, not mathematical scoring:

- **65% industrial / brutalist structure**
- **20% editorial luxury and commerce clarity**
- **15% surreal / crimson tension**

If the interface becomes confusing, theatrical or art-project-like, the surreal component is too strong.

If it becomes a generic monochrome luxury shop, the surreal/crimson component is too weak.

## 5. External references and transferable observations

### 5.1 Balenciaga — Raw Architecture / brutalist retail

Official sources:
- https://www.balenciaga.com/es-es/chicago-store-opening
- https://www.balenciaga.com/es-es/ginza-tokyo-flagship-store-opening
- https://www.balenciaga.com/es-es/greene-street-new-york-flagship-store-opening
- https://www.balenciaga.com/it-it/madrid-store-opening
- https://www.balenciaga.com/es-es/savoir-faire

Transferable patterns:
- intentionally untreated / raw surfaces;
- concrete, cement board, metal and exposed technical systems;
- rough edges and visible structure;
- liminal indoor/outdoor atmosphere;
- warehouse / studio cues;
- hard rectangular partitions;
- modern luxury created through tension between raw infrastructure and refined fashion;
- restraint around the product so architecture does not distract from the collection.

Use these as **material and spatial metaphors** for a web layout.

Do not reproduce Balenciaga identity, site chrome, campaign language or proprietary content.

### 5.2 Balenciaga current ecommerce

Official source:
- https://www.balenciaga.com/

Transferable patterns:
- campaign-first entry;
- low-copy product discovery;
- clear collection/category calls;
- high image-to-text ratio;
- restrained commerce vocabulary.

Use the hierarchy, not the branding.

### 5.3 LEMAIRE

Official source:
- https://www.lemaire.fr/

Transferable patterns:
- product-first merchandising;
- disciplined category taxonomy;
- restrained labels;
- editorial and commerce modules coexisting without excessive decoration;
- generous product imagery.

Use for commerce clarity and restraint.

### 5.4 Jacquemus

Official source:
- https://www.jacquemus.com/

Transferable patterns:
- strong campaign-to-product transition;
- confident hero framing;
- clear product blocks;
- commercial content that remains fashion-led.

Use for rhythm and product emphasis.

## 6. Inheritance from Quiet Atelier v1.0

Preserve the parts of the first implementation that worked:

- editorial hierarchy;
- large fashion imagery;
- warm bone/ivory surfaces rather than pure white;
- serif display + sans utility contrast;
- horizontal New arrivals and Best sellers rails;
- direct CTA language;
- generous negative space where it supports hierarchy;
- border-led structure rather than shadow-heavy cards;
- accessible keyboard focus;
- no unnecessary JavaScript.

Industrial Maison Rouge **adds pressure and structure**; it does not discard ecommerce clarity.

## 7. Structural translation

### Header
Read as a thin black technical bar: centered brand, compact navigation, search/account/cart utilities.

### Hero
Read as an architectural campaign wall:
- full-width;
- raw-concrete environment;
- subject offset to the right;
- text-safe dark zone to the left;
- crimson sculptural fabric/form crossing the frame;
- one dominant heading;
- one primary CTA.

### Product rails
Read as inventory displayed inside a gallery or industrial rack:
- large 4:5 images;
- hard edges;
- compact metadata;
- no floating cards;
- no rounded containers;
- crimson appears as a small action/status accent, not a large card fill.

### Editorial/category bridge
Read as three/four architectural apertures:
- one dominant image crop;
- hard vertical divisions;
- brief labels;
- category taxonomy remains canonical.

### Footer
Read as a black technical/plinth surface with clear columns and a fine border grid.

## 8. Surrealism contract at reference level

Surrealism should appear through:
- scale tension;
- improbable but elegant crimson fabric/form;
- asymmetric cropping;
- body/architecture relationships;
- slightly uncanny fashion poses;
- spatial interruption.

Surrealism must **not** appear through:
- distorted UI;
- illegible typography;
- text rotation that damages reading;
- random collage;
- psychedelic gradients;
- melting objects;
- excessive image manipulation;
- animated chaos.

## 9. Crimson contract at reference level

Crimson is the signature interruption.

Preferred role:
- campaign asset;
- primary CTA;
- micro-accent;
- hover/focus;
- a selected editorial textile/object.

Crimson is not:
- the default page background;
- the default body copy color;
- a substitute for information hierarchy;
- a color applied to every product.

## 10. Anti-reference / prohibited drift

Reject implementations that resemble:
- generic luxury Shopify templates;
- black-and-red gaming websites;
- cyberpunk/neon interfaces;
- glassmorphism;
- soft rounded SaaS cards;
- gothic ornament;
- maximalist surreal collage;
- Balenciaga imitation;
- brutalism that compromises usability;
- monochrome minimalism with no crimson/surreal tension.

## 11. Minimum fidelity test

A reviewer should be able to identify, without being told:

1. raw / industrial structure;
2. fashion editorial hierarchy;
3. a precise crimson signature;
4. a small surreal tension;
5. clear ecommerce behavior.

If any one of items 1–4 is absent, visual fidelity is incomplete.
If item 5 is absent, the design fails regardless of visual fidelity.
